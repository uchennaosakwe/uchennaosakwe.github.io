How to use this app

Five minutes here pays for itself. Tap any section to open it.

First-time setup

1. Open Settings (the gear, top right) and set your three shares (living / savings / investing). Your budget itself is built from income you log as it arrives, plus anything carried over from last month — so it grows with each payment rather than assuming one payday. The expected-income box is just a reference figure.

2. Check the accounts list matches how you actually pay (add or remove accounts freely), and glance at the USD and NGN exchange rates so foreign entries convert sensibly.

3. In Chrome on your phone, tap the browser menu and choose Add to Home screen. Tally then opens from its own icon like any app.

4. Work through the three platform checklists below once — they set up your Trading 212 ISA, Trove, and emergency-fund account the cost-efficient way.

The five-second logging habit

The app lives or dies on one habit: log the moment you pay, while your phone is still in your hand. The green button is under your thumb on purpose — tap it, type the amount, tap a category chip, tap Save. Date, time and your last-used account are pre-filled.

Add the merchant in the note field when you can ("Tesco", "TfL", "LemFi") — it powers the Top Merchant insight and makes month-end exports far more useful. Tap any logged transaction to edit or delete it, so a mistyped amount is never a problem.

Paid in dollars or naira? Tag the currency on entry and the app converts it to pounds everywhere using your Settings rates.

Reading the Home screen

Safe to spend is your headline: your living share of this month's available money minus everything living-related you've spent, with a per-day figure for the days remaining. If the card's number is healthy, you're winning; if it turns rose, the month needs a quieter second half.

The three pacing bars compare each bucket to where you should be at this point in the month. Living costs warn as they approach the 40% ceiling; savings and investments tell you whether you're on pace to hit their targets — for those two, filling the bar is the goal, not the danger.

The three small cards above the rule show how the budget was built: income logged this month, what was carried over from last month, and the available total everything is budgeted from. Log a payment at any point in the month and all three, plus every bar below, move immediately.

The highlights row is your weekly mirror: the biggest single expense of the last seven days, the merchant taking most of your money this month, and your combined saved-plus-invested total against its 60% target.

Reading Stats

The donut shows where this month's outflow actually went, by category — tap a slice for its exact figure. The cash-flow chart runs September 2026 to October 2027: the green income bar against the stacked outflow bar (bills, discretionary, savings, investments) for every month. Watching the cyan and indigo segments grow month after month is the whole journey in one picture.

Daily burn compares what you actually spend on discretionary things per day against what you can afford per day for the rest of the month. It's the single most honest number in the app — check it before a weekend, not after.

One nuance worth knowing: burn rate counts only discretionary categories (eating out, shopping, entertainment, personal, other), so logging groceries as Groceries rather than Shopping keeps it accurate.

Running the investment side

Two tabs, two jobs. Invest is the overview: your totals, where money sits across UK, US, global, Nigerian and cash buckets, and this month's deployment plan — the weights split your 40% investment bucket into exact pound amounts per sleeve, so payday requires no arithmetic. Keep the weights totalling 100.

Portfolio is the ledger. Add each purchase once (name, ticker, platform, market, currency, price, units), then update the current value whenever you check your platforms — monthly is plenty; daily checking mostly teaches anxiety. Gains and the pound conversion recalculate instantly.

Note the two systems are deliberately separate: logging an expense to "Trading 212 ISA" records the cash leaving your budget; adding a holding in Portfolio records what it bought. Do both — one feeds the budget, the other tracks the asset.

The month-end ritual

On the last day of the month (or the first of the next): export the CSV from Settings, paste it into the companion workbook or Google Sheets if you use them, then tap Start a fresh month. A safety CSV downloads automatically, the month's totals are archived so the year chart keeps its history, and the log clears for a clean start. The investment side is never touched by a reset.

While you're there: update your holdings' current values, refresh the USD/NGN rates, and give the rollover a thought — any living-budget underspend is real money you can sweep into savings or investments.

Keeping your data safe

Everything lives in this browser on this phone — private by design, but it means clearing the browser's site data erases Tally, and nothing syncs between devices by itself. The defence is cheap: export a JSON backup from Settings every week or two and let it sit in your Downloads or Drive. Restoring it on any device brings back everything — transactions, archive, holdings, settings, even checklist ticks.

The JSON is the full clone for restoring; the CSV is the readable one for spreadsheets — and it imports back in too: Import transactions from CSV adds rows without touching what you already have, skipping anything already logged, so the same file can be re-imported safely. Keep both at month-end and you can never lose more than a few days.

The passcode screen keeps casual eyes out of the app, but the data on the device is not encrypted — treat the passcode as a privacy screen, not a vault.

Getting the most out of it

Pay yourself first. Move the savings and investment shares out on payday — log them, watch those two bars fill on day one — then live on what's left. Budgeting what remains is far easier than saving what remains.

Automate the boring transfers. Standing order to savings, AutoInvest on Trading 212, both dated the day after payday. Tally then becomes a place you record decisions, not a place you rely on willpower.

Batch the Nigeria leg quarterly so fixed transfer fees stay tiny next to the amount — let its share of the monthly bucket accumulate as cash until it's worth sending.

Review weekly, act monthly. A two-minute Sunday glance at Home and daily burn catches drift early; save the bigger decisions (weights, categories, rates) for month-end so you're steering, not twitching.

Be honest with categories. The app only ever reflects what you tell it — a takeaway logged as Groceries flatters this month and misleads every chart after it.
